What a CGI ad is

A product ad with no shoot

A CGI product ad is built digitally instead of filmed on a set. The product sits in scenes that would be slow or expensive to film: liquid splashes, floating layers, water, macro close-ups and camera moves that glide around the bottle.

For brands that sell on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, that means a premium-looking ad without booking a studio, a crew or a photographer. It also means new versions are quick: a different background, a new colour, a seasonal cut or a different hook for testing.

Skincare and beauty brands use it most, but it works for anything with a recognisable shape: hair oil, serums, bags, earbuds, burgers. Each CGI ad example in the collection has its own page with the idea behind it and why it works.

Brands use CGI commercials the way they once used studio product video production: for launches, seasonal campaigns and always-on paid ads. The style is especially popular with US ecommerce and DTC brands selling on Instagram, TikTok and Amazon.

FAQ

Quick answers

What is a CGI ad?

A product ad created digitally instead of filmed on set. Think liquids, splashes, exploding layers and camera moves that would cost a full studio day to shoot.

Do I need to send the product?

No. Clear photos of the product and its label are enough to build the shots around it.

Do you make UGC ads?

Yes. UGC ads are edited from creator footage into scroll-stopping ads with a hook in the first second, captions, sound and a clear call to action. UGC video ads and UGC-style ads work best next to CGI ads: UGC builds trust, CGI makes the product look premium.

Do you also edit videos?

Yes. Think of it as hiring a short form video editor and a freelance video editor for ads in one. Short form video editing for Reels, Shorts and TikTok, with captions, sound and hooks, from your own footage or from scratch. That includes a reels editing service, TikTok video editing service, YouTube Shorts editing and video ad editing for UGC ads.

Which brands are CGI ads good for?

Any product with a clear shape: a CGI skincare ad, CGI beauty ads and CGI cosmetics ads are the most common, followed by CGI hair care ads, CGI food ads, CGI fashion ads and CGI luxury product ads for bags and accessories. They work especially well as CGI ads for ecommerce and CGI ads for Shopify brands.

CGI ads vs UGC ads: which should I use?

Use both. UGC ads build trust with a real person talking. CGI ads make the product itself look premium and stop the scroll. Many brands run a CGI product video as the hook and UGC as the follow-up. Both can be edited here.

What is FOOH?

FOOH means fake out of home: a CGI ad that shows a product appearing in a real street scene, like a giant bottle on a building. FOOH advertising is a style of CGI marketing made for social media. If you want a FOOH ad, mention it on the first call.

Can CGI ads run on Instagram Reels and TikTok?

Yes. These are CGI ads for Instagram Reels and CGI ads for TikTok first, in vertical 9:16. They also work as Meta ads for skincare brands and other ecommerce products.

Can you make a product ad without a photoshoot?

Yes. That's the point of a CGI product ad. The scenes are created digitally around clear photos of your product, so there's no studio, no crew and no shipping samples.

What kinds of products work best?

Products with a clear shape and label: skincare and serums, hair care, cosmetics, bags and accessories, food and drinks, and small electronics like earbuds. The collection above shows each of these.

Which formats do I get?

Vertical 9:16 for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok as standard, plus square or wide versions if your ads or website need them.

Can the same ad run as a paid ad?

Yes. The ads are made to work with the sound off and to earn attention in the first second, which is what paid placements on Meta and TikTok need. If you want, the campaign can be run too. See Paid Ads.
